      Why it uses current balances

      A card's current balance is every charge you haven't paid yet. The statement balance stops at the closing date, which for a card due on the 9th is around the middle of last month, so it misses the last few weeks of spending. Subtracting current balances covers those weeks too, and you never have to work out which charge lands on which statement. It treats every card like a debit card.

      What happens to the money you leave in checking

      1. 1stRent and Bank of America are already paid. Enter today's balances and move the result to savings.
      2. 9th–12thAmex Platinum, Amex Gold and Amazon autopay their statement balances from the money still in checking.
      3. Next 1stCharges made after those statements closed are still covered by what you left behind. Run it again.

      Tips

      • Set every card's autopay to the full statement balance.
      • If an app lists pending charges apart from the balance, add them to that card's number.
      • A card payment that's still pending can show up in both checking and the card. Wait for it to clear, or count it once.
      • When you cancel the Amex in March, delete its row after the final payment posts.
